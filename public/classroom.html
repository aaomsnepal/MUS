Nepal Stock Exchange — clear map for common people

NEPSE is Nepal’s only stock exchange. Companies list shares; the public can buy/sell through licensed brokers using the TMS system. This page is educational — not buy/sell advice.

Rules (hours, circuit limits, settlement) can change. Always confirm with NEPSE, SEBON, CDSC or your broker before trading.

1. Accounts you need

  • Broker account — licensed stock broker
  • Demat account — holds shares electronically (via depository participant)
  • MeroShare — CDSC portal for IPO apply, holdings view, some corporate actions
  • TMS login — broker’s Trading Management System to place secondary-market orders
  • Bank link — for funds in/out with the broker

MeroShare is not the same as TMS: IPO/holdings vs live secondary trading.

2. Primary vs secondary

  • Primary (IPO / FPO / rights) — company issues new shares; apply via MeroShare when open
  • Secondary (NEPSE board) — buy/sell already listed shares with other investors during market hours
  • Bonus / right / dividend — corporate actions; credit timing follows CDSC schedules, not same-day trading logic

3. Trading day structure (typical)

  • Pre-open — orders can be entered for price discovery before continuous trading
  • Regular session — about 11:00–15:00 NPT continuous matching (check current NEPSE circular for exact minutes)
  • Order-driven market — price–time priority; you trade against other orders, not a market-maker quote board like some foreign systems

Weekend pattern shifted with national calendar changes in 2026 (toward Mon–Fri open in recent SEBON/NEPSE updates). Confirm the current holiday calendar on nepalstock.com.np.

4. Settlement (T+2 idea)

Equity trades generally settle on a T+2 cycle: trade day T, shares/funds complete about two business days later via CDSC clearing.

  • You usually cannot sell what has not settled yet
  • Same-day buy→sell “intraday style” is not how classic NEPSE cash equity is designed
  • Broker charges, SEBON fee and DP charges still apply — small trades feel expensive

5. Circuit breakers & bands

Two different ideas:

  • Stock price band — a single scrip can only move within a daily % limit from the reference price (historically often discussed around ±10%; verify current SEBON/NEPSE rule)
  • Index circuit — if the NEPSE index moves sharply, the whole market can pause or stop for the day

April 2026 updates discussed a 5% pause / 8% day-stop style index rule. Always read the latest circular — do not trade on memory of old 3/4/5% tiers.

6. Indices people watch

  • NEPSE Index — broad market, market-cap weighted (base history from the 1990s)
  • Sensitive / Float / sector indices — narrower views (banks, hydro, insurance, etc.)
  • Index up does not mean every stock is up — check your scrip and sector

7. Sectors on NEPSE

Common groups investors sort by:

  • Commercial banks · Development banks · Finance · Microfinance
  • Life / non-life insurance · Hydropower · Manufacturing & processing
  • Hotels & tourism · Trading · Others · Mutual funds / promotions as listed

Sector heat can differ a lot from the main index on the same day.

8. Words on the board

  • LTP — last traded price
  • Open / High / Low / Close — session range
  • Volume / turnover — how much traded (qty vs rupees)
  • % change — vs previous close reference
  • Floorsheet — record of matched trades
  • Market depth — visible buy/sell order levels in TMS

9. How this site helps (and what it is not)

  • Screener — real session list from public market publishers (refresh, not broker ticks)
  • Classroom slides — memory tools for price-action ideas
  • NEPSE & economy news — auto headlines linked to the publisher
  • Not a broker, not TMS, not personalized advice, not a guaranteed signal service

Practice reading structure on the NEPSE screener. Keep a notebook of your own rules.

10. Safety habits

  • Never average a falling story without a written plan
  • Size positions so one mistake cannot ruin the account (risk % rule)
  • IPO allotment ≠ instant tradable liquidity in every case — know lock/credit timing
  • Ignore “sure tips” on social media; verify filings and your own risk
  • Tax, broker fees and opportunity cost matter as much as chart patterns

Educational content only. Capital is at risk in equity markets.
